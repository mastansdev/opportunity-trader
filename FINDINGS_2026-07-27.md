## 2. Why the bot stopped trading — three causes, all by design

1. **53% of its historical trades are shorts**, and the regime gate
   blocks every short in a bullish tape. Half its normal candidate flow
   disappears on exactly the days the operator likes most.
2. **It needs a FRESH cross of the opening range.** On a trending day a
   stock breaks out once, early, then keeps climbing — it never crosses
   again, so it never signals again.
3. `ONE_TRADE_PER_SYMBOL_PER_DAY` + `BLOCK_REENTRY_AFTER_STOPOUT`
   permanently retired both of today's names by 09:32.

**Entry timing across 61 sessions:**

| Hour | Trades | Share |
|---|---|---|
| 09:00 | 834 | 42.6% |
| 10:00 | 704 | 36.0% |
| 11:00 | 201 | 10.3% |
| 12:00+ | 218 | 11.1% |

79% of everything it has ever done happens before 11:00. **It is an
opening-range scanner, not a trend follower.** The operator's stated
rule — trade with the trend — is not what the machine implements.

## 4. The cost arithmetic

Measured over 61 sessions, 1,957 trades:

```
GROSS     +Rs   67,996      (+Rs 35 per trade)
CHARGES   -Rs  228,792      (Rs 117 per trade, 336% of gross)
NET       -Rs  160,796      (-Rs 2,636 per session)
```

Of that ₹117, roughly **₹70 is statutory** — STT, exchange, stamp, SEBI,
GST. Not negotiable by any broker.

**With a zero-brokerage broker this system still loses ₹35 a trade.**
STT alone exceeds the average gross. That kills "find a cheaper broker"
as a strategy.

Costs measured as a share of what is risked:

| Setup | Risk | Stop | Charges | % of risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intraday, ₹800, 0.4% | ₹800 | 0.4% | ₹118 | **14.7%** |
| Swing, 1% risk, 5% stop | ₹30,000 | 5% | ₹267 | **0.89%** |

The cost problem is a **holding-period** problem. The same ₹117 buys a
0.3% intraday wiggle or a 4% multi-day move.

## 6. The bug I shipped

`backtest/swing_replay.py` reported results four times with confidence.
It had a **look-ahead bug**: positions still open at the end of a window
were sold at the symbol's LAST EVER price — five years in the future.

```
BSE   in 2021-12-03 @ 212.88   out 2021-12-31 @ 3,549.70   20 days
INFY  in 2021-12-29 @ 1,883.50 out 2021-12-31 @ 1,040.90    2 days
```

₹3,549.70 is BSE's 2026 price. The results were inflated roughly **4×**.

**It was found because the operator asked to see individual trades
instead of accepting a summary.** No summary statistic revealed it.

Fixed, and pinned by `tests/test_swing_replay_no_lookahead.py`. The bug
was deliberately reintroduced to confirm the tests fail, then removed.

**Standing rule from this: show trades before summaries.**

## 7. The missed stop — operator-found, live

SWIGGY traded below its stop on TradingView. The bot held on.

`core/trailing_stop.py`'s `is_hit()` is `price <= stop` — it can only see
prices that ARRIVE. The Dhan feed sends periodic snapshots, not every
trade. A dip through the stop between two snapshots is invisible.

This project already proved the feed samples (ZENTEC seen 1784.20 vs a
real 1792.00, which is why the ORB reconcile exists). Nobody had applied
the same reasoning to the stop.

**Consequence: `RISK_PER_TRADE_RS` was never actually enforced.** It was
the risk taken IF the feed caught the breach, unbounded if it did not.
Every replay in this repo assumes stops fill at the stop price.

Fixed by reconciling against the exchange's own day extreme, which
`circuit_monitor` already polls every ~3 seconds. A day low only falls;
if it extends past our stop after entry, the breach is ours and we
missed it. Tagged `MISSED_STOP_RECONCILED` so they can be counted.

## 9. THE RESULT THAT MATTERS: the financial data exists

The operator's actual strategy is: **only buy when results are better
than the previous quarter.** The bot had no financial data of any kind —
`results_calendar.db` records WHEN results were filed, never WHAT the
numbers were. Every test above substituted price behaviour for financial
quality, which is why they all measured shadows.

**Checked both exchanges:**

| Source | Verdict |
|---|---|
| `nse.results_comparison()` | **stale** — newest quarter 31-DEC-2024, for every symbol |
| `nse.financial_results()` | **stale** — all 3,814 rows dated 31-Dec-2024 |
| **`bse.resultsSnapshot()`** | **LIVE and verified** |

Verified against an independent source the operator supplied:

| | BSE returned | Operator's card |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue Jun-26 | 3,049.42 | 3,049 |
| Revenue Mar-26 | 2,903.65 | 2,904 |
| Net Profit Jun-26 | **755.70** | **756** |
| Net Profit Mar-26 | **724.96** | **725** |
| EPS Jun-26 | 7.82 | 7.8 |

Free, already installed, current quarter, already in crores.

```python
from bse import BSE
b = BSE("data")
b.resultsSnapshot(b.getScripCode("KARURVYSYA"))
```

**Limitation:** returns only three periods — latest quarter, previous
quarter, full year. No same-quarter-last-year. The API takes only
`scripcode`, so history cannot be walked backwards.

**The operator's solution, and it is correct:** store all three every
time we poll. By July 2027 the memory holds Jun-26, and year-on-year
becomes available without any extra source. The memory builds itself.

Poll **weekly**, not quarterly — companies report across a two-month
window and a quarterly poll would leave permanent holes.

**Caveat on quarter-on-quarter:** it is misleading for seasonal
businesses. A festive-season company beats its previous quarter every
October and misses every January regardless of how it is doing. This is
exactly why year-on-year matters and why the wait is worth it.
